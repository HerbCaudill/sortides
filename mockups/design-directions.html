Sortides – design directions

Five directions and a phone sketch for the shared outings site, all showing the same fictional week of 26 October 2026 in Barcelona. Compare shape, not content. “Sortides” is a placeholder name.

Why: A weekly magazine page that the agent edits: one pick of the weekend, a few more with reasons, and evergreen places on the side – best for browsing together on the sofa.

Tradeoff: It shows the agent’s choices and hides the rest, so finding a specific thing or anything beyond this week takes a search.

Why: Where you are leads: a map of Catalonia with drive-time rings from Barcelona or Tamariu, so day trips and nearby events sit side by side – best for “what can we reach today?”.

Tradeoff: Maps are poor at time; a concert on Thursday and a museum open until March look the same, and a dense city cluster needs zooming.

Why: A dated agenda for the next ten days plus a shelf of places sorted by how much time they take – best when you sit down to plan which evenings to use.

Tradeoff: Evergreen outings get the side column, so the inventory you’re building feels secondary to the event listings.

Why: You say where you are, when you’re free and what you feel like, and it deals three options you can reshuffle – built for breaking the rut rather than browsing.

Tradeoff: It hides the list on purpose, so it doesn’t give you the satisfying sense of a growing collection, and it needs good tags on every item.

Why: One dense, sortable table of every item with faceted filters and full keyboard control – the most honest view of the inventory and the easiest to check what agents have added.

Tradeoff: It reads like a database rather than an invitation; Lynne is less likely to open it just for ideas.

Why: The same data as a pocket app with What’s on, Outings, Movies and Saved tabs – the version you’d actually pull out on a Saturday afternoon.

Tradeoff: Any desktop direction above would need its own phone layout; this one most closely follows A.
